## 1. SFT as Stage 1 of every post-training pipeline

SFT = next-token training on **(prompt → ideal answer)** examples, with loss on the answer. Every later stage (DPO, ORPO, GRPO, PPO) assumes SFT already taught the model to follow instructions **in your format**.

```text
loss = − Σ over ANSWER tokens t of  log P(token_t | prompt, answer tokens before t)
```

```powershell
python labs/lab50_sft_stage1.py
```

**YOU SHOULD SEE (real output):**

```text
A) WHAT SFT CHANGES: the model learns the behaviour shown in the data
model              accuracy on trained prompts   accuracy on unseen prompts
base (random)                           0.3333                          0.5
after SFT                                  1.0                          0.0
B) WHAT SFT CANNOT DO: teach a fact that is not in the training data
  base       prefers 'severity three'
  after SFT  prefers 'severity three'  (it has no information about this case: it is guessing in the learned FORMAT)
```

**Read this twice:** SFT taught the trained prompts perfectly (1.0) and generalised **worse** to unseen prompts (0.0): too few, too narrow examples → memorisation. And SFT cannot invent a fact that is absent from the data: it answers in the learned **format** with a confident guess. That is how fine-tuned models "hallucinate in your company's tone". Facts come from pre-training, from retrieval (RAG), or from data that actually contains them.

```text
  one 2-turn example: 49 tokens, 6 trained (12%)
  the loss sees only: 'severity two<|im_end|>severity three<|im_end|>'
```

**Chat fine-tuning checklist:**
- one example = one **conversation**, not one message pair;
- **every** assistant turn trained, every user/system turn masked;
- vary the system prompt in training, or the model only works with the exact one you used;
- include conversations that end with a refusal, a clarifying question, or "that is not in the documents";
- include long conversations: training on 2 turns and serving 10 puts later turns out of distribution;
- keep the serving template **byte-identical** to the training template.

## 4. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) fine-tuning

Put the intermediate steps **in the assistant target**, so the model learns to produce them and then conditions its final answer on its own steps. Reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3 thinking mode) are trained on long traces, then RL (T05 GRPO).

```powershell
python labs/lab53_cot_finetuning.py
```

**YOU SHOULD SEE (real output):**

```text
answer only        train acc 1.000   held-out acc 0.889   mean answer tokens 2.0
chain of thought   train acc 0.889   held-out acc 0.556   mean answer tokens 14.0
Honest reading: a 3-layer randomly initialised model on 18 examples. It is a demonstration of the
mechanics and the COST, not evidence about CoT quality.
```

What transfers regardless of the toy:
- CoT targets are **3-5× longer** → 3-5× the training tokens and the generation cost per answer;
- traces must be **correct**: training on wrong reasoning that reaches the right answer teaches fake reasoning (keep only traces whose final answer verifies);
- distilling a strong model's traces into a small one transfers some reasoning ability (the R1-distill models), but check the teacher's licence/terms;
- if you only need the answer, train answer-only and pay for reasoning at inference.


**YOU SHOULD SEE (real output):**

```text
A) HOW MUCH DOMAIN DATA? (4 machines AND a phrasing never seen in training, chance 0.25)
 training rows  subjects  templates   held-out accuracy
             4         1          1                0.25
            32         4          2                0.25
            96         6          4                0.75
B) DOES DOMAIN-ONLY TRAINING COST THE OTHER CAPABILITY?
stage 1: domain only                            0.75            0.6667
stage 1: domain + 20% replay                   0.625               1.0
...
fine-tuned on DOMAIN only                  2.987        0.000   general WORSE (+2.983)
fine-tuned on DOMAIN + 25% replay          0.000        0.000   general ok
```

**The 10 rules (each one measured in a lab or a paper):**

1. **Coverage beats volume:** every entity × intent × phrasing your users produce (lab 54: 96 diverse rows beat 32 narrow ones).
2. **Hold out ENTITIES, not just rows:** the real test is unseen machines/customers/products/patients.
3. **Mix 5-30% general or previous-capability data ("replay")** to prevent catastrophic forgetting (lab 17, lab 54 B).
4. **Vary the system prompt; include refusals and "not in the documents".**
5. **Start at lr 1e-4 - 3e-4, r = 16** on a real model with LoRA (2e-5 is for full fine-tuning).
6. **1-3 epochs**; evaluate every 20-50 steps; keep the **best** checkpoint, not the last.
7. **Evaluate per segment:** an average hides the segment your biggest customer cares about.
8. **Re-run a general suite every time.** A domain gain that costs general ability is usually a bad trade.
9. **Behaviour in SFT, facts in retrieval.** If facts change monthly, fine-tune the model to **use** retrieved context (cite it, refuse when missing), and retrieve the facts.
10. **Read 30 random rows** of your training data before every run. It is the cheapest debugging there is.
